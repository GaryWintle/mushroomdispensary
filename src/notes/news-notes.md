## 5. Arc 3 — The August 2024 Court Ruling ⚠️ MISSING FROM SITE ENTIRELY

**This is the most important addition.** The current page ends in January 2024 — it doesn't reflect that the city _lost_ the legal fight seven months later. This is your strongest "third-party validation" story (a judge's own words ruling the city failed to prove its case) and it's currently invisible to anyone reading this page.

All four sources confirmed, dated as the source actually states:

- **[Court rules against City of Vancouver in mushroom dispensary crackdown](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/suspected-mushroom-dispensary-court-decision-1.7306210)** — CBC News (Yasmine Ghania), **August 28, 2024**
- **[Vancouver mushroom dispensary back open after winning court ruling](https://globalnews.ca/news/10724342/vancouver-mushroom-dispensary-court-ruling/)** — Global News BC, **August 28–29, 2024**
- **[Court rules against Vancouver in mushroom dispensary crackdown](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/court-rules-against-vancouver-mushroom-110000308.html)** — Yahoo News Canada, **August 28, 2024**
- **[Court Rules Psychedelic Mushroom Dispensary Can Remain Open in Vancouver](https://doubleblindmag.com/vancouver-mushroom-dispensary-wins/)** — DoubleBlind Magazine, **September 12, 2024** — bonus find, not in the original library. This one's worth featuring on its own: it has Larsen telling the outlet directly, _"We will outlast the current mayor and council... we're not going anywhere and in the end, we are going to win."_ DoubleBlind is a niche but well-regarded psychedelics-industry publication, so it adds a different kind of credibility than the mainstream outlets — worth a "as covered in the psychedelics press too" angle.

**Important nuance for accuracy:** the ruling wasn't a clean acquittal. The court found the _director of the operating non-profit_ (Cindy Heemeryck of the Strathcona Tea Society) guilty of a bylaw offence (unauthorized use of premises), but separately found the City had failed to prove the business sold products containing psilocybin specifically. Both CBC and Yahoo frame it accurately as "found guilty of a bylaw violation, BUT the city couldn't prove the psilocybin claim" — your copy should keep that distinction rather than overstating it as a full win. The "fantastic... we were really hoping for this kind of victory" Larsen quote and the judge's "possibility or probability doesn't meet the standard of proof" line are both still completely accurate and usable — just be precise about what was and wasn't decided.

## 6. Arc 4 — The 2024 Licence Saga (March–November 2024) ⚠️ ALSO MISSING

This turned out to be more of a multi-round back-and-forth than a single clean "reinstatement" event — worth knowing the real shape of it so the page doesn't overclaim. Here's the actual sequence, each dated as the source states:

1. **[Vancouver re-instates magic mushroom shop's business licence](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/psilocybin-business-licence-hearing)** — Daily Hive, **March 6, 2024**. A city Business Licence Review panel voted 2-1 (Green Party councillors Adriane Carr and Pete Fry in favour) to overturn the 2023 suspension and reissue the licence, with terms framing the business as "education and advocacy regarding entheogens." Larsen quoted as "cautiously optimistic" going in. This is the moment that matches the "city-government endorsement" framing from your library.
2. **[Vancouver magic mushroom dispensary gets business licence back](https://globalnews.ca/news/10339230/vancouver-magic-mushroom-dispensary-licence-back)** — Global News BC, **March 6, 2024**. Same event, second source — also notes Mayor Ken Sim publicly condemned the panel's decision (useful as the "even after winning, there's still political opposition" beat).
3. **[Vancouver's chief licence inspector orders magic mushroom dispensary to close](https://www.squamishchief.com/bc-news/vancouvers-chief-licence-inspector-orders-magic-mushroom-dispensary-to-close-8642412)** — Squamish Chief / Castanet (Postmedia network), **April 2024**. The city's chief licence inspector pushed back, arguing the reinstated 2023 licence didn't authorize 2024 operations and ordering closure again — referred the 2024 application to full council instead.
4. **[No magic at city council for Vancouver mushroom dispensaries as retail framework rejected](https://globalnews.ca/news/10414859/magic-mushroom-vancouver-retail/)** — Global News BC, **April 11, 2024**. Council rejected a proposal to create a regulatory framework for psilocybin retail generally — a month after the panel win for this specific business.
5. **[Vancouver magic mushroom dispensary gets business licence re-instated](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/vancouver-magic-mushroom-dispensary-gets-business-licence-re-instated/)** — CTV News, **November 20, 2024**. The licence was reinstated again later in the year — this appears to be the resolution of the back-and-forth from steps 1–4.

**How I'd frame this on the page:** not as one tidy "we won our licence back" moment, but as the _pattern_ — "throughout 2024 the city tried multiple times to shut this down through licensing, and each time it didn't stick." That's actually a stronger and more honest narrative than a single reinstatement story, and it dovetails with the "we'll outlast the current council" quote from the DoubleBlind piece above. If you want one featured link for this section, I'd pick #1 (Daily Hive, March 6) for the strongest single quote, with #5 (CTV, Nov 20) as the "and it stuck" closer.
